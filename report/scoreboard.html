How each matcher works 4 matchers
  • Identity matcher, strict: Nick's event-sourced identity matcher (C-057) at its own setting: accepts a look-alike only when it is at least 90% sure; otherwise sends it to human review.
  • Identity matcher, relaxed: The same matcher, but it accepts a look-alike at a confidence you set (default 85%).
  • ID-first rules: Trusts the member or subscriber ID on the card; uses name, birth date and gender only to break ties. A common way to build member match.
  • Check-every-field rules: Finds the card's ID, then insists that name, birth date and gender all agree, even when the ID already picked one person.

What the results mean 8 results
✓ Found the right person
One member was the person in the request, and the matcher returned that member.
✓ Rightly refused: not this person
The card and the person don't agree, so nobody should be returned. The matcher refused (HRex 422).
✓ Rightly refused: can't tell apart
More than one member fits the request equally. HRex says refuse (422), and the matcher did.
✓ Rightly refused: consent
The consent sent with the request can't be honoured, so HRex says refuse (422). The matcher did.
✗ Wrong person
The matcher returned a member who is not the person asking, or returned someone when it should have refused. This is how one person's records reach another.
✗ Ignored consent
The matcher returned the right person despite a consent the plan can't honour.
✗ Missed the right person
One member was the right answer, but the matcher refused. Safe, but the member's records don't move.
✗ No usable answer
The matcher crashed or answered in a form that isn't a valid $member-match response.

HRex says a plan returns at most one member, and refuses (HTTP 422) when there is no match, more than one match, or a consent it can’t honour. Any refusal where a refusal is due counts as right.
